Juniper Resilience Global Risk Matrix, 2026 Edition

This matrix synthesizes 91 open-access data sources across 15 risk domains to produce standardized 1–5 risk ratings for 215+ countries/territories and 500+ major cities. All sources are government agencies, UN/IGO bodies, academic institutions, or established NGOs. No private company data is used.

Rating Scale

1 · Very Low
Minimal risk. Strong institutional capacity.
2 · Low
Below-average risk. Generally well-managed.
3 · Moderate
Meaningful risk. Enhanced due diligence warranted.
4 · High
Significant risk. Active mitigation required.
5 · Critical
Extreme risk. Exceptional controls required.

City-Level Methodology

City scores use parent country scores as baseline, adjusted for city-specific data: EIU Safe Cities Index 2024 (60 cities, 76 indicators) · Numbeo Crime Index 2025 (500+ cities, continuous, Pietermaritzburg highest 82.0, Abu Dhabi lowest) · ACLED sub-national 2025 (city-level conflict proximity) · GEM Global Seismic Risk Profiles (city earthquake exposure) · EM-DAT city-level disaster frequency 2024. City weights: Security 25%, Geo/Pol 20%, Terror 15%, Infrastructure 15%, Medical 10%, Environmental 8%, Scams 5%, Cyber 4%.

Airline Safety & Security (2026 starter dataset)

159 airlines are scored on two dimensions, each on the same 1 to 5 scale used across the matrix. The Safety Score weights accident history and JACDEC risk (25), ASN hull-loss and serious-incident rate (20), IATA IOSA registration (15), EU Air Safety List status (15), FAA IASA category of the certifying state (10), average fleet age (10) and years in operation (5). The Security Score weights OFAC, EU and UN sanctions exposure (30), historical security incidents including hijackings, shoot-downs and illicit cargo (20), route-network exposure (20), state ownership and government ties (15) and the basis of any EU restriction, where a security or sanctions-driven ban scores higher than a safety-only listing (15).

Route exposure is calculated from each airline's approximate country network, using this matrix's country ratings: 40% the certifying state's country composite and 60% a network score of 1 + 2 × (share of countries rated moderate) + 4 × (share rated high or critical), capped at 5. Reports show the count of low, moderate and high-risk countries served and name the high-risk ones. The Airline Composite combines Safety (55%) and Security (45%). Engagement flags apply the composite plus hard triggers: any carrier with comprehensive sanctions designation is flagged Avoid regardless of score, any EU listing or major sanctions exposure is at least Caution, and any carrier based in a high or critical risk country is at least Monitor.

Ground Transport (2026)

Every city and country receives a Ground Transport Score on the same 1 to 5 scale. It weights street crime and security in the location (30), taxi and rideshare safety (20), road safety from WHO road death rates (20), public transport suitability (10), traffic congestion (10), GPS reliability including jamming and spoofing (5), and availability of vetted drivers as a mitigation (5). Flags are Clear below 2.25, Monitor below 3, Caution below 4 and Avoid at 4 or above. The movement planner estimates drive time from a city-wide minutes-per-10-km figure for peak, midday and evening, informed by the TomTom Traffic Index, then adds a security buffer (10% to 50% plus 5 to 20 minutes for pickup and vehicle checks) that scales with the location's flag. Any location in a country with active armed conflict is at least Caution, and any location with no safe transport option without security support is Avoid.

Rideshare platforms, recommended navigation apps and GPS interference reflect public coverage information as of October 2026. Congestion levels are informed by the TomTom Traffic Index. Taxi guidance, public transport suitability and armoured vehicle availability are Juniper Resilience assessments, not a published dataset, and should be confirmed with local security providers before a deployment.

October 2026 data refresh

All country, city, NGO, ground transport, airline and payment data were refreshed on 2 October 2026 against the most recent public releases. Key inputs: the 2026 Global Peace Index (June 2026), FATF plenary outcomes (19 June 2026: Iraq and Bosnia and Herzegovina added to the grey list, Algeria and Namibia removed), CIVICUS Monitor ratings (People Power Under Attack 2025, data December 2025), U.S. State Department advisories (September 2026), WHO, ECDC and UKHSA outbreak monitoring (September 2026), and the OFAC and State Department Syria sanctions relief (Caesar Act repeal December 2025, State Sponsor of Terrorism rescission 24 August 2026).

Main score changes: the 2026 Iran war (from 28 February 2026, with the ceasefire collapsing on 8 July) raised geopolitical, conflict, security and terrorism scores for Iran, Israel, Iraq, Jordan and the Gulf states struck by Iranian missiles and drones (UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia; Oman to a lesser degree). The 2026 Lebanon war raised Lebanon to the top band. Pakistan's open war with Afghanistan since February 2026 raised its conflict and geopolitical scores. Coups and attempts in Madagascar, Guinea-Bissau and Benin raised geopolitical scores. Syria's sanctions score fell from 5 to 3. Civic space ratings were aligned to the latest CIVICUS ratings for every rated country, which corrected a number of outdated values. The Medical domain, which previously held a placeholder of 1 for every country, was rebuilt from health-system capacity and active outbreaks, including the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in the DRC (a Public Health Emergency of International Concern since 16 May 2026). Ground transport, airline route exposure and payment guidance were recalculated from the refreshed country scores. A full change log accompanies this release.

Government travel advice

Each country report and briefing shows the whole-country travel advice level from six governments, normalised to a 1 to 4 scale: the United States (official advisory feed, 2 October 2026), Australia (Smartraveller, all destinations, 2 October 2026), the United Kingdom (FCDO whole-country warnings, checked 2 to 3 October 2026), Canada (formal advisories, including the 26 June 2026 Middle East changes), France (countries formellement déconseillés in full) and Japan (MOFA Level 3 and 4 notices for the Middle East). Where a government's level was not captured for a country, the report says so and links to the official page rather than assuming a level. The matrix was cross-checked against these levels; Oman's security score was raised to reflect U.S. and Australian Level 3 advice.

Country Briefing

The Country Briefing consolidates every module for one country: the 15-domain country composite and top risks, the NGO composite and civic space, matrix cities with their ground transport flags and typical trip times, airlines certified in the country and airlines that serve it, and practical essentials. Emergency numbers, plug types, voltage, currency, dialling codes, time zones, languages and tap water guidance reflect public reference information as of October 2026 and should be confirmed locally. Police reliability is derived from the matrix's corruption and security scores. Payment guidance (card-first, mobile-first, mixed, cash-first or cash only) reflects card acceptance, local mobile payment systems and sanctions that block foreign cards, as of October 2026. Embassy notes cover U.S. representation in detail; for other nationalities the briefing links to the official government directory.

Scores represent strategic-level risk assessments for planning purposes and are not a substitute for real-time operational intelligence.
